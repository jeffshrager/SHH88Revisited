## The check pass, and what it changed
A read of the paper against the Lean sources found one gap in the main argument.

**The theory's fan effect depends on the size of the space.** The draft compared the idealized
model at n = 2^16 with the game at n = 8 and blamed the tree idealization (short cycles) for
the r = 0 discrepancy. That does not follow:

- at n = 8 and 16 the idealized model has no fan effect at r = 0 or at r = ½
  (`SHH.GD.no_fan_small`, new; integer-diameter settings); the prototype says the same between
  those settings and at n = 32, the size of the 1988 simulations; a slight rise first appears
  near n = 64 (36 → 39.8 at μ = 3, r = 0);
- so at the game's size, theory and game agree at r = 0 (neither has a fan effect) and
  disagree at r = ½ (the game has one, `SHH.BitGame.figure1_pattern_three`; the theory does
  not);
- the 1988 Figure 2 used n = 10,000, where the walk does show the pattern, plus an r = 0 rise
  up to about μ = 5 that the simulations do not show.

The paper now says the walk does not explain the game's fan effect, and that what does is
open. Abstract, introduction, Section 5, Table 3, Discussion, Limitations and Conclusion were
redrafted; CLAIMS T6–T10 rewritten to match.

Smaller corrections in the same pass:
- Eq. 5's proviso μ(1−r) ≫ 1 is stated; the r = 0 sentence of 1988 carries no proviso.
- "only from μ = 16 on do more edges help" was wrong: the peak at n = 2^16 is near μ = 6.
- The Heathcote sentence said diminishing relative returns is "what the data most clearly
  show"; Heathcote et al. argue the reverse for individual learners. Reworded.
- `curveGD_not_powerLaw` needs (1 + ε)·r < 1; the paper's "any exponent and tolerance" now
  says so.
- "32 operators that could be learned" are 32 start–goal pairs, 20 distinct new operators.
- Table 1's r = ½ column is certified exactly (`SHH.GD.fan_pow2_half_values`, new), so the
  uncertified 257 is gone. Also new: `SHH.GD.DOf_pow2_gen`.
- `tools/walk_check.py` prints the size dependence and the small-space values;
  `tools/bitgame.py` reports distinct operators.
